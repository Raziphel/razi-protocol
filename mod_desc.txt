# Welcome to Razi Protocol 3.0

Razi Protocol brings a huge collection of Space Age planet and content mods into one connected campaign. You'll explore seven star systems, establish industries across dozens of worlds, and build the research and transport networks needed to reach the farthest corners of the galaxy.

The journey starts on Nauvis, but the factories you build there are only the beginning. Each star system has its own research identity and a central **System Tech Card** that brings its planets' discoveries together. As new destinations open, older worlds keep their place in the campaign through science, resources, and production.

There's more to the expedition than unlocking the next planet. The built-in **Expedition Journal** tracks quests, milestones, and large construction projects. Curated routes help you navigate the galaxy, while the Dyson Sphere, the Transceiver, and the journey into Deep Space lead toward the final challenge: **Convergence**.

Razi Protocol connects the original mods rather than replacing them. Their planets, recipes, machines, and unique mechanics are still the heart of the experience. Razi brings them into a shared progression.

# Please Read Before Starting

* **Start a new save for the intended 3.0 experience.** Upgrading from 2.0 may be possible, but existing saves can need manual fixes. Make a backup first.
* **Check the Dependencies tab.** Space Age, Krastorio 2 Spaced Out, and the required companion mods form the intended campaign.
* **Progression changes from the beginning.** This is not just an extra endgame added after vanilla Space Age.
* **Normal is the recommended difficulty.** Easy, Hard, and Nightmare are available, along with more detailed settings.
* **Some settings require a restart.** World-generation changes won't alter already-generated terrain, and major progression changes can affect existing saves.
* **Keep your old factories running.** Earlier planets continue to contribute resources, technology, and production as the expedition grows.
* **Use the Expedition Journal.** Press **Ctrl + Shift + J** to find objectives, projects, and recorded milestones.
* **3.0 is still in development.** Features, balance, and compatibility may change before release.

# Explore the Galaxy

Razi Protocol organizes its worlds into **seven major star systems**, each with its own industries, research, and place in the expedition. The starmap gives those systems a deliberate layout, with curated routes and visible danger levels so you can plan a journey instead of guessing where to go next.

Your first foothold is **Calidus**. From there, the galaxy opens into several branches. Some systems offer alternative routes, but the research and logistical work needed to reach the outer regions still matters.

* **Calidus:** The beginning of your expedition, from Nauvis and Muluna to the early foundations of the Dyson Sphere.
* **Nyxaris & Solaris:** Two major branches that introduce new materials, industries, and research opportunities.
* **Vespera:** Specialized worlds and routes that put more demands on your transport network.
* **Vibrant:** A wider range of planetary industries and resources to bring into the galactic economy.
* **Umbra & Beetlejuice:** Outer-system expeditions with greater hazards and more demanding infrastructure.
* **Beyond the seven systems:** Bellicos, Aegis, Deep Space, and the path to Convergence.

Planets aren't designed to be one-time stops. Each system's discoveries feed into its central Tech Card, while the resources and factories you've built continue to support later expeditions. Route colors, travel information, and system groupings make the larger galaxy easier to navigate without taking away the challenge of exploring it.

# Make the Galaxy Yours

Razi Protocol has a lot of moving parts, and you shouldn't have to play the exact same way as everyone else. Its settings let you adjust the challenge, the pace of industrial growth, and how much visual detail appears on your starmap.

Start with **Easy, Normal, Hard, or Nightmare**. Normal is the intended balance, while the other presets give you a different challenge without making you configure every setting individually. If you want finer control, there's plenty to adjust:

* **Difficulty & Threats:** Enemy density, evolution, expansion, raids, pollution, fortifications, and Nexus threats.
* **Resources & Industry:** Ore frequency, patch size, richness, spoilage, research costs, rocket costs, and factory throughput.
* **Worlds & Encounters:** Habitats for supported Armoured, Cold, Explosive, Toxic, Arachnid, and Electric enemies.
* **Asteroids & Belts:** Asteroid populations, belt hazards, ore richness, salvage, and quality asteroids.
* **Industrial Integration:** Cross-world trade recipes, planetary dependencies, technology distribution, and supported transport routes.
* **Stellar Projects:** Dyson Sphere and HELIOS progression, construction options, and stellar hazards.
* **Navigation & Quality of Life:** Route colors, brightness, tooltips, map information, and recipe visibility.
* **Galaxy Visuals:** Star halos, nebulae, dust, orbit rings, and other background details.
* **Performance:** Optional reductions to particles, fragments, smoke, and other demanding effects.

You can leave everything at its recommended defaults and play the intended campaign, or adjust individual systems to suit your preferences. The more experimental performance options are optional, not requirements.

**Important:** Some settings take effect only after restarting Factorio. Back up your save before making major changes to progression, recipes, or world generation.

# The Expedition Journal

With so many planets, technologies, and long-term projects, it's easy to lose track of what you were working toward. The **Expedition Journal** brings your progress together in one place, without making you follow a rigid checklist.

It has three main sections: **Quests, Projects, and Systems**.

## Quests: Follow Your Expedition

Your journey is divided into chapters with milestones tied to research, exploration, factory development, and logistics. You can review completed objectives, see what's still open, and revisit the history of your expedition.

* **Chapter Progress:** Follow the campaign from early industry on Nauvis toward Deep Space.
* **Practical Objectives:** Advance through things you actually research, build, or discover.
* **Milestone History:** See completed objectives alongside your remaining goals.
* **Next Steps:** Find a useful direction when you're not sure what to work on next.

## Projects: Build Something Bigger

Some goals take more than finishing a research queue. Project dossiers track the major engineering and exploration work behind the campaign:

* **First Space Platform:** Establish your first platform beyond Nauvis.
* **Operation Beltbreaker:** Prepare for dangerous asteroid crossings.
* **Dyson Sphere:** Pursue the research and construction of a major stellar project.
* **The Transceiver:** Work toward access to Deep Space.
* **The Ninefold Signal:** Face the nine Nexus bosses through their encounters.
* **Project Ark:** Prepare an expedition beyond the established systems.
* **The Convergence:** Bring your research, factories, and logistics together for the final challenge.

Research opens these projects, but the practical work still has to be done. The Journal helps you track that work without pretending a technology unlock is the same as a completed structure.

## Systems: See What's Happening

The Systems tab shows readouts from supported companion mods while leaving their original controls intact.

* **Fleet Logistics:** Available Planet Express information about your interplanetary cargo network.
* **Zero-Point Energy:** Supported Nexus reactor infrastructure and construction limits.
* **Nexus Environment:** Environmental conditions, shielding, and stabilizers where the source mod exposes that data.
* **Live Readouts:** Useful status information gathered into one convenient screen.

The Journal is there when you want direction and stays out of the way when you'd rather explore. Your completed chapters and projects remain part of the expedition's record.

**Open the Expedition Journal with Ctrl + Shift + J.**

# Science & Technology

Razi Protocol's technology tree is more than a collection of extra research from individual planet mods. It ties their discoveries into the campaign, giving you reasons to develop several worlds and keep their industries connected.

## Seven Systems, Shared Research

Every major star system has a **System Tech Card**. The planetary research within that system feeds toward the card, turning discoveries from several worlds into a milestone for the larger expedition.

* **Planetary Research:** Explore each world's original technology and production chains.
* **System Tech Cards:** Bring the discoveries of a star system together under a shared research milestone.
* **Connected Progression:** Use discoveries from earlier systems to reach technologies farther out in the galaxy.
* **Industrial Specialization:** Keep different planets useful for the materials, science, and manufacturing they provide.
* **Long-Term Goals:** Build toward the technologies and infrastructure needed for Deep Space.

## Research Beyond One Planet

Progress doesn't end when you unlock a new destination. As the research tree expands, you'll need supplies and production from more than one world. Establishing a new factory matters, but so does keeping your existing network supplied.

The aim isn't simply to make everything cost more. It's to give research a logical place in the expedition, so that planetary discoveries and their associated industries remain relevant.

## From Science to Stellar Engineering

Later research supports the campaign's largest projects, from the Dyson Sphere and Transceiver to the technology needed for Convergence. Those unlocks are important, but completing the projects still requires real construction, transport, and operation.

Razi Protocol builds on the original mods' research rather than stripping away what makes each one different. The science tree should give you a reason to explore the galaxy, and a reason to keep the worlds you've already explored working together.

# The Razi Protocol Expedition Wiki

A galaxy this large deserves a proper field guide.

The [Razi Protocol Wiki](https://wiki.razisrealm.com/factorio) is a companion to your expedition, with practical information for finding your next destination, understanding the research behind it, and keeping a complex modpack running smoothly.

* **Getting Started:** Installation, dependencies, save compatibility, and what to expect from the campaign.
* **Galaxy Atlas:** The seven star systems, their planets, major routes, and the journey toward Deep Space.
* **Science & Tech Cards:** How planetary discoveries fit into the system-wide research progression.
* **Projects & Logistics:** Guidance for major construction goals, factory networks, and the infrastructure you'll need later in the campaign.
* **Settings & Survival:** Difficulty, world hazards, asteroid travel, customization, and performance options.
* **Field Guides & Troubleshooting:** Help with progression blockers, missing worlds, mod compatibility, and safe updates.
* **Expedition Notebook:** A personal checklist for keeping track of your plans. Notebook notes are stored in your browser; they don't read or change your Factorio save.

The in-game **Expedition Journal** tracks what your factory has actually accomplished. The wiki is where you can read ahead, look something up, or figure out why a plan isn't working.
